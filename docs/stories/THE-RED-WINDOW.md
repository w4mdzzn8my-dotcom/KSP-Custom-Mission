# The Red Window

Proposed routine science/social scene for the Duna expedition, by Basil's Codex.
This is a workshop draft for Hamish and Basil, not settled character canon or a
playable mission. It adds no emergency. It assumes an eventual qualified Duna
orbit and a human watch; it cannot establish either condition itself.

## Starting situation

Hamish has promised the relief crew a picture of the first landscape they can
recognise together. Basil wants a useful observation record, with the view,
vessel position and lighting identified. The first image shows a bright patch
near the limb. Hamish calls it frost; Basil thinks it may be cloud or glare.
Neither has enough evidence. Ada and Vikram remain in their scheduled cryosleep.

At the command window, Hamish moves his notebook out of the light.

“First postcard?”

“First question,” Basil says. “Keep the original.”

This is a disagreement about interpretation, not an instrument failure. The
scene must not spawn a damaged part or award a discovery for choosing a line.

## Work aboard

1. At the command station, read Duna, vessel identity, UT and the actual orbital
   state. Save the original player-taken image with that context. A future
   instrument must expose real state; a dialogue choice cannot supply it.
2. At the science station, compare the retained-report inventory with its earlier
   capture. Existing inventory comparison can describe stored subjects and data
   changes, but cannot identify the bright patch. No stock science is granted.
3. Divide the follow-up: Hamish watches for another useful view while Basil
   checks whether the geometry and lighting actually allow a comparison. They
   can swap roles. Observations must fall within the current human watch; no
   maneuver, thaw or shift-clock change is scheduled to force a repeat view.
4. If another view occurs during ordinary work, record its real context and
   compare the images. Otherwise leave a question for the relief watch and
   continue the mission. A missing opportunity is a valid outcome, not a long
   idle countdown or permission to accelerate an unqualified coast.
5. In the galley, choose the caption together. “Bright patch near the limb” is
   acceptable when the cause remains uncertain. The original, comparison and
   uncertainty travel together in the handover.

“You can still call it beautiful,” Hamish says.

“I was going to.” Basil turns the notebook so they can both see it.

## Workload, supplies and recovery

Target 12–18 minutes of active play across instrument reading, short interior
travel, image comparison and conversation, interleaved with other useful duty.
No observation interval alone satisfies the scene. Use existing Food/Water for
any chosen meal; do not invent a free meal reward. Normal ship power, cryo
reserves and crew eligibility remain authoritative.

A wrong caption can be revised without losing the original observation. If
someone is tired or occupied, reduce the task to one recorded view and a clear
handover question. No crew member is forced awake or automatically made fit.

## Implementation and acceptance still needed

Existing read-only orbit/report displays supply only some context. A persistent
observation notebook, image/context binding, scene trigger, dialogue state and
an in-ship presentation are future work. Test save/reload of both the original
and revision; in LMP, verify both clients see the same record and author identity.
Physical window visibility, actual Duna orbital observations and any scientific
identification require separate evidence. The scene never certifies a landing,
completed experiment, repair, route or two-human approval.
